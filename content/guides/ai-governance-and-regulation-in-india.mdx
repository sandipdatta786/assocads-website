People often ask whether India has an AI law. The short version is that AI in India is governed through a mix of existing laws, newer rules aimed at AI-generated content, sector regulators and government guidance. Knowing which is which matters, because only law and binding rules create legal duties.

This guide sorts the main instruments into three groups: what is law, what is guidance, and what is still proposed. It is general information, not legal advice. Take advice from a qualified lawyer on how any of this applies to your own work.

## Is there a single AI law in India?

No AI-specific statute was found in the official sources checked for this guide. The government's stated approach is to work through existing laws. When MeitY released the [India AI Governance Guidelines](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2186639) on 5 November 2025, its Secretary said the focus would remain on using existing legislation wherever possible.

## What is law today?

| Instrument | Status | How it touches AI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information Technology Act, 2000 | Law | Offences such as identity theft, impersonation and privacy violation; blocking and takedown powers |
| IT Rules, 2021, as amended on 10 February 2026 | Binding rules | Due diligence by intermediaries, including rules on AI-generated audio, images and video |
| Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and DPDP Rules, 2025 | Law, coming into force in phases | Any AI system that processes digital personal data |
| Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 | Law | PIB cites sections 353 and 111 as usable against harmful deepfakes |
| SEBI regulations on AI tools | Binding regulations | Regulated entities answer for the AI tools they use |

### The IT Act and the IT Rules

A [PIB release of 30 July 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2291671) lists the IT Act provisions the government relies on against deepfakes. These include sections 66C (identity theft), 66D (impersonation), 66E (privacy), 67 and 67A (obscene and sexually explicit content), 69A (blocking orders) and 79 (intermediary liability).

On 10 February 2026, the [IT Rules, 2021](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/550681ab908f8afb135b0ad42816a1c9.pdf) were amended by G.S.R. 120(E). The amendment added a definition of **synthetically generated information** in Rule 2(1)(wa). It covers audio, visual or audio-visual content created or altered by a computer so that it appears real and could be taken for a real person or event. Routine, good-faith editing is excluded.

According to the [PIB summary of 25 March 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2245053), the amended rules require intermediaries to:

- use reasonable technical measures to stop users creating or sharing unlawful AI-generated content;
- label permitted AI-generated content clearly and attach traceable metadata;
- warn users about the legal consequences of unlawful AI-generated content; and
- remove unlawful content within three hours of a court order or a reasoned intimation from the government.

Significant social media intermediaries have extra duties under Rule 4(1A), including asking users to declare whether content is synthetically generated and checking that declaration.

### The DPDP Act

The [Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf) applies to any AI system that processes digital personal data, including when building training datasets. Under the DPDP Rules as published, most of its duties apply from May 2027; a proposal reported in 2026 to bring this forward for some organisations had not been notified when we checked. Under the DPDP Rules, a significant data fiduciary must check that algorithmic software it uses is not likely to risk individuals' rights. Our guide [the DPDP Act explained for data teams](/guides/dpdp-act-explained-for-data-teams) covers this in detail.

### Sector regulators

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has required regulated entities to report their AI and machine learning systems since 2019. In December 2024 its Board considered a [proposal](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/meetingfiles/dec-2024/1735042007618_1.pdf) to amend its regulations so that market institutions and intermediaries are responsible for the AI tools they use. In February 2025 SEBI notified the change for intermediaries as Regulation 16C of the SEBI (Intermediaries) Regulations, 2008, with a matching change for stock exchanges and clearing corporations.

## What is government guidance?

Guidance shapes expectations and may later inform rules, but it does not by itself create legal duties.

- **MeitY advisories.** PIB lists advisories to intermediaries dated 26 December 2023, 15 March 2024, 29 December 2025, 9 February 2026 and 16 March 2026. The 15 March 2024 advisory dealt with tools that can create or alter text, audio and video. Advisories remind platforms of duties that already exist under the IT Act and Rules.
- **India AI Governance Guidelines (November 2025).** Drafted by a committee chaired by Prof. B. Ravindran of IIT Madras. They contain seven guiding principles, recommendations across six pillars, an action plan and practical guidelines for industry and regulators. They also propose new bodies, including an AI Governance Group, a Technology and Policy Expert Committee and the AI Safety Institute. Check MeitY's announcements for which of these have been set up.
- **NITI Aayog's responsible AI papers.** [Part 1](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2021-02/Responsible-AI-22022021.pdf) (February 2021) set out seven principles: safety and reliability, equality, inclusivity and non-discrimination, privacy and security, transparency, accountability, and protection of positive human values. [Part 2](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2021-08/Part2-Responsible-AI-12082021.pdf) (August 2021) covered how to put them into practice.
- **RBI's FREE-AI report.** On [13 August 2025](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=61018), the Reserve Bank of India released a committee report on responsible and ethical AI in the financial sector, with 7 sutras, 6 pillars and 26 recommendations. It is a committee report, not a direction to banks. Check the RBI's website for any directions issued since.

## What is still proposed?

- **SEBI's guidelines on responsible AI and machine learning.** SEBI published a [consultation paper](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jun-2025/1750415065695.pdf) in June 2025 proposing guiding principles for AI use in securities markets. Check SEBI's website for whether they have been finalised.
- **Further changes to the IT Rules.** Reports refer to further draft amendments on labelling published by MeitY in 2026. Check MeitY's website for their current status.
- **The action plan in the governance guidelines**, which sets out short, medium and long-term steps rather than laws already in force.

## What are the main international reference points?

- **EU AI Act.** [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai) entered into force on 1 August 2024. It sorts AI systems by risk: some practices are banned, high-risk systems face strict duties, some systems carry transparency duties, and most face no specific rules. Bans applied from 2 February 2025 and rules for general-purpose AI models from 2 August 2025. After a 2026 amendment, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, rules for high-risk uses listed in Annex III apply from 2 December 2027.
- **OECD AI Principles.** [Adopted in 2019 and updated in May 2024](https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles), they set five values-based principles, including transparency, robustness and accountability, and five recommendations for governments.

Indian organisations that sell to or serve users in the EU may need to follow the EU AI Act as well as Indian law.

## What should organisations do now?

1. **List your AI systems** and note which laws touch each one, especially the DPDP Act and, for platforms, the IT Rules.
2. **Check for AI-generated media.** If your product creates or hosts synthetic audio, images or video, review the February 2026 IT Rules amendment.
3. **Follow your sector regulator**, such as the RBI or SEBI, for directions specific to your industry.
4. **Use the guidance as a benchmark.** The governance guidelines and NITI Aayog's principles show what the government expects, even where they are not binding.
5. **Watch for change.** Rules in this area are changing quickly, so check the date of every source you rely on.
6. **Take legal advice** on how these rules apply to your own systems.

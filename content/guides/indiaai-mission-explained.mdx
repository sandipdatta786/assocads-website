The IndiaAI Mission is the Government of India's main programme for building national capacity in artificial intelligence. It pays for shared computing power, Indian foundation models, open datasets, skills and research. People often see large figures about it in the news, so it helps to separate what was approved in 2024 from what has been delivered since.

Every figure in this guide comes from the Press Information Bureau (PIB) or an IndiaAI website, and each carries the date it refers to. Government programmes change often, so check the latest official releases before relying on a number.

## What did the Cabinet approve, and when?

On [7 March 2024](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2012355), the Union Cabinet approved the IndiaAI Mission with a budget outlay of ₹10,371.92 crore. PIB releases in 2026 describe this outlay as spread over five years. Some releases round it to ₹10,371 crore or about ₹10,372 crore; the approved figure is ₹10,371.92 crore.

The 2024 release said the mission would be run by the IndiaAI Independent Business Division under Digital India Corporation, a body under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY).

## What are the seven pillars of the IndiaAI Mission?

The names of the pillars have changed slightly since 2024. The table uses the names from the 2024 Cabinet release and the names used by PIB in 2026.

| Pillar (2024 name) | Name used in 2026 | What it was approved to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| IndiaAI Compute Capacity | IndiaAI Compute | Build AI compute of 10,000 or more GPUs through public-private partnership, plus an AI marketplace |
| IndiaAI Innovation Centre | Foundation Models | Develop indigenous large multimodal models and domain-specific foundation models |
| IndiaAI Datasets Platform | AIKosh | Give startups and researchers one place to access quality non-personal datasets |
| IndiaAI Application Development Initiative | Same | Build AI applications for problems set by ministries and state departments |
| IndiaAI FutureSkills | Same | Expand AI courses and set up Data and AI Labs in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities |
| IndiaAI Startup Financing | Same | Help deep-tech AI startups reach funding |
| Safe and Trusted AI | Same | Fund responsible AI projects, indigenous tools, self-assessment checklists and governance frameworks |

## What has been delivered on compute?

The 2024 plan was for 10,000 or more GPUs. PIB's [factsheet of 13 August 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?id=150860&NoteId=150860&ModuleId=16) reported shared compute capacity of more than 45,000 GPUs as of June 2026. These are provided by private compute service providers selected by IndiaAI, rather than owned by the government.

According to a [PIB release of 6 August 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295477), 15 compute service providers had been empanelled over four rounds. By August 2026, 237 projects had received subsidised compute, with 93.18 lakh GPU hours sanctioned. The same release says a purchase order had been issued for a government high-performance AI compute system of about 1.1 exaflops at the National Informatics Centre data centre in Delhi. ## What has happened with Indian foundation models?

IndiaAI issued a call for proposals on 30 January 2025 and received 506 applications. PIB reported in August 2026 that 20 proposals had been selected for support: 12 large multimodal models and 8 small language models. The applicants keep the intellectual property.

The same release names outputs already launched, including models from Sarvam AI, Gnani.AI, BharatGen and Avataar AI. This guide lists them only because the government release does; it does not assess or recommend any of them. ## What is AIKosh and how big is it?

[AIKosh](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/) is the IndiaAI platform for datasets, AI models and use cases. It also offers a sandbox and tutorials for developers. It is the delivered form of the Datasets Platform pillar.

PIB reported that AIKosh hosted more than 14,000 datasets and 331 AI models as of July 2026. When this guide was checked on 23 November 2026, the AIKosh home page itself showed more than 16,263 datasets and 386 models. Counts like these measure what is listed, not the quality or licence of each item, so check those before using a dataset.

## What about skills, applications and research centres?

PIB gave the following figures in releases of [27 July 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2289946) and 6 August 2026:

- **Fellowships:** 686 fellowships awarded across undergraduate, postgraduate and doctoral programmes in 178 institutions.
- **Data and AI Labs:** 27 India Data and AI Labs set up, with work under way on 188 more.
- **Applications:** 62 AI prototypes developed and 20 AI solutions deployed in public-sector institutions.
- **Hackathons:** the 6 August release and the August factsheet say 11 national hackathons or innovation challenges; the 27 July release says 12.
- **Centres of Excellence:** 58 AI Centres of Excellence approved across states and union territories, of which 22 had been approved and started in 13 states and union territories.

## What is the Safe and Trusted AI pillar doing?

PIB reported in July 2026 that 13 responsible AI projects had been approved at educational institutions. They cover bias mitigation, machine unlearning, privacy-preserving AI, explainability, deepfake detection and AI risk assessment. The IndiaAI Safety Institute was announced in January 2025 and, according to the same release, has been set up to work through a hub-and-spoke model with academia, startups, industry and government.

This pillar sits alongside the India AI Governance Guidelines, released by MeitY in November 2025. Our guide on [AI governance and regulation in India](/guides/ai-governance-and-regulation-in-india) explains how they fit together.

## How can students, researchers and startups take part?

1. **Explore AIKosh** for datasets and models, and check each item's licence and documentation.
2. **Read the calls for proposals** on [IndiaAI](https://indiaai.gov.in/), which is where compute access, model funding and challenges are announced.
3. **Look for FutureSkills opportunities** such as fellowships and Data and AI Labs at institutions near you.
4. **Follow PIB releases from MeitY** for updated figures, and note the date of every figure you quote.

An internship is often the first time a student works with real data inside an organisation. It teaches things a course cannot: messy data, deadlines, and explaining results to people who need to act on them. It can also lead to a job offer, though there is no guarantee of that.

This guide covers where to look, how to apply and what to check before you accept. It does not recommend or rank any company or platform.

## Where can you look for an AI or data science internship?

Search several channels at the same time. Each reaches a different set of openings.

- **Your college placement or training cell.** Many companies recruit interns through colleges. Ask about the timetable and eligibility rules early.
- **Faculty and research projects.** Professors in computer science, statistics, economics and other departments often need help with data analysis. Research internships at other universities and research institutes are another option.
- **Company career pages.** Many employers list internships on their own websites before or instead of on job boards.
- **Government portals.** Two national portals are described below.
- **Commercial job and internship platforms.** Several private platforms list internships. Read each listing carefully and check the organisation independently.
- **Open source programmes.** Contributing to open source projects builds skills and a public record of your work.
- **Your own network.** Seniors, alumni, teachers and people you meet at public talks and meetups can tell you about openings that are never advertised.

## What is the AICTE National Internship Portal?

The [National Internship Portal](https://internship.aicte-india.org/) is run by the All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE) under the Ministry of Education. It describes itself as a free platform for students of AICTE, UGC, IGNOU and other institutions, and says that registration, applications, shortlisting and completion certificates cost students nothing.

When we checked on 23 November 2026, the portal showed an "AI / ML & Data" category, with listings from government bodies, public sector organisations, companies and training organisations. Listings change often, so check the details of each one, including who is offering it, whether it is paid and what you will actually do.

## What is the PM Internship Scheme, and does it include AI or data roles?

The Prime Minister's Internship Scheme (PMIS) is run by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. According to a [government factsheet of 21 March 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/may/doc2025522558101.pdf), it was launched on 3 October 2024 with the aim of giving one crore young people 12-month paid internships in leading companies over five years. At the time of that factsheet it was in a pilot phase.

The same factsheet lists the sectors of participating companies, and "IT & Software Development" is among them. However, the scheme is not specific to AI or data work, and we could not confirm how many of its internships involve AI or data science. Use the portal's filters to see what is currently offered.

In April 2026, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs [expanded eligibility](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/government-expands-eligibility-of-pm-internship-scheme-to-include-final-year-students/) to final-year undergraduate and postgraduate students aged 18 to 25. They must submit a no-objection certificate from their institution confirming that the internship will not interfere with their studies.

The scheme pays interns monthly financial assistance, but its other eligibility rules and the amount may have changed since the pilot began. Always check the [official portal](https://pminternship.mca.gov.in/) before applying.

## What do you need before you apply?

Prepare these before you start applying, so you can respond quickly when an opening appears.

1. **A one-page CV.** List your course, relevant subjects, tools you can use (for example Python and SQL) and your projects. Keep it factual.
2. **Two or three documented projects.** Each should state the question, the data, the method, the result and its limits. Put the code in a public repository with a short guide to running it.
3. **A short note for each application.** Say why you are interested in this organisation and which of your projects is most relevant. A tailored note of a few sentences is better than a long generic letter.
4. **References.** Ask a teacher or project supervisor in advance whether they are willing to be contacted.

## How do you make your application stand out?

Most applicants for data internships list the same courses and tools. What differs is evidence.

- **Show, do not list.** A link to a clear project analysis says more than a list of libraries.
- **Use relevant data.** If you are applying to a healthcare organisation, a project on public health data shows interest and initiative.
- **Be accurate.** Describe only work you did yourself. Interviewers often ask about every project on a CV.
- **Write clearly.** Data roles depend on explaining results. A well-written application is itself evidence.

## How do you prepare for an internship interview?

Expect a mix of basic technical questions and questions about your own work. Common areas include probability and statistics, SQL queries, Python data handling, the basics of machine learning and model evaluation, and a walk-through of one of your projects.

Some organisations set a short take-home task. Treat it like a small project: state your assumptions, explain your choices and note the limits of your answer.

## What should you check before accepting an offer?

Before you accept, make sure you know:

- **Who you will work for** and who will supervise you.
- **What you will do.** Ask for a short description of the work. Be cautious of internships that are mostly sales or data entry if you want analytical experience.
- **Duration, hours and location**, including whether the internship is remote.
- **Pay, if any**, and when it is paid.
- **Whether you will receive a certificate or letter** describing your work.
- **Your college's rules**, if the internship is to count for academic credit.

Never pay a fee to be given an internship. Be wary of offers that ask for money for "training", "registration" or "certification" before you can start, and check any organisation you do not know through its official website and other independent sources.

## What if you do not get an internship this year?

Many students do not get an internship on their first attempt. You can still build experience through an independent project on public data, a contribution to an open source project, or a research task with a faculty member. Programmes such as [Google Summer of Code](https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com/), a global online programme in which new contributors work on open source projects with mentors for 12 weeks or more, are one option; check its own pages for eligibility and dates.

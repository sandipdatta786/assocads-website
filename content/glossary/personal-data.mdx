## In plain English

Personal data is information about a person who can be identified from it, either directly or when it is combined with other information. A name, phone number, address or photograph is personal data, and so can be a customer number or device ID that links back to someone. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (the DPDP Act) mainly governs personal data in digital form, including data collected on paper and later digitised.

The Act uses two key terms. The data principal is the individual the data is about; for a child, this includes the parents or lawful guardian. The data fiduciary is any person or organisation that decides why and how the personal data is processed. A data processor is someone who processes it on a fiduciary's behalf.

## An example

A coaching institute in Kolkata keeps an online register of students' names, mobile numbers and test scores. Each student is a data principal. The institute, which decides what the data is collected for and how it is used, is the data fiduciary. If it hires a software company to run the register, that company acts as a data processor. Under the Act, the institute remains responsible for how the data is handled.

## Why it matters

Almost every AI and Data Science project touches data about people, from customer records to training data scraped from the web. Knowing what counts as personal data is the first step in handling it lawfully and respectfully.

The Act sets out obligations for data fiduciaries and rights for data principals, and it has exceptions, such as data processed for purely personal or domestic purposes. This entry is general information, not legal advice. Organisations should read the Act and any rules made under it, and take qualified advice for their own situation.

## In plain English

Data analytics means looking carefully at data to answer practical questions. What sold most last month? Which areas have the longest waiting times? Is this year different from last year?

Analysts clean the data, summarise it, compare groups and present the results in tables, charts and dashboards. Some analytics looks back to describe what happened; some looks forward to estimate what is likely to happen next.

Data analytics overlaps with data science. Analytics usually focuses on answering defined business or policy questions with existing data, while data science more often involves building new models and methods.

## An example

A chain of pharmacies in West Bengal wants to avoid running out of common medicines during the monsoon. An analyst combines two years of sales records by store and week, then charts how demand for items such as oral rehydration salts changes with the season. The analysis shows which stores see the sharpest rise, and the purchasing team uses it to plan stock earlier.

## Why it matters

Almost every organisation collects data, and analytics is how most of them actually use it. For students it is a common entry route into AI and Data Science careers, needing skills in spreadsheets, SQL, statistics and clear communication. For businesses and government bodies, sound analytics replaces guesswork with evidence, as long as the data is accurate and the results are explained honestly, including their limits.

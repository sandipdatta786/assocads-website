## In plain English

Natural language processing, or NLP, is the part of AI that deals with ordinary human language, the kind we speak and write every day. It covers tasks such as translation, speech recognition, summarising documents, answering questions and sorting messages by topic.

Human language is hard for computers because the same word can mean different things, meaning depends on context, and people write informally. Modern NLP mostly relies on deep learning models trained on large amounts of text and speech, including large language models.

India's many languages make NLP especially important. The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology runs the National Language Translation Mission, Bhashini, which aims to support translation between Indian languages and English using AI and NLP.

## An example

A state government grievance portal receives complaints in Bengali, Hindi, Santali and English. An NLP system translates each complaint, identifies the department it concerns, and flags urgent words such as those about water supply failures. Officials still read and act on every complaint, but they receive them already sorted, which lets them respond faster.

## Why it matters

Most of the world's information is in language, not tables, so NLP opens up a large share of data to analysis. For government bodies in India, it can help deliver services in the languages people actually use. For businesses, it supports customer service and document handling. For students, NLP is an active field where Indian languages still need much more work, especially for languages with less digital text available.

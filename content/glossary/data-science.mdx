## In plain English

Data science is about turning raw data into knowledge that people can act on. A data scientist collects and cleans data, explores it to see what it shows, tests ideas against it, and often builds models that predict or explain something.

It draws on three areas: statistics and mathematics, programming and computing, and an understanding of the field the data comes from, such as health, retail or transport. Without the third, the numbers can easily be misread.

NIST describes data science as a way of gaining knowledge directly from data, either by open exploration or by forming a hypothesis and testing it.

## An example

A municipal corporation wants to know why some wards report far more waterlogging complaints than others during the monsoon. A data science team combines complaint records, rainfall data and drainage maps. They explore the data, test whether complaints rise with particular drainage features, and build a simple model that estimates which streets are most at risk after heavy rain. Engineers use the results to decide where to inspect first.

## Why it matters

Organisations of every kind now hold large amounts of data, but data alone does not answer questions. Data science is the discipline that does. For students it is a broad career path with roles in almost every sector. For businesses and government bodies it supports decisions based on evidence rather than habit, provided the work is done carefully and its limits are stated honestly.

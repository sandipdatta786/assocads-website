## In plain English

Responsible AI means thinking about the effects of an AI system on people, not only about whether it works technically. It asks questions such as: is it accurate for everyone, or only for some groups? Can people find out why it made a decision? Who is answerable if it goes wrong?

In India, NITI Aayog's 2021 paper set out seven principles for responsible AI, including safety and reliability, equality, inclusivity and non-discrimination, privacy and security, transparency and accountability. NIST in the United States lists similar qualities of trustworthy AI.

Responsible AI is not a single tool. It is a set of habits applied from the design of a system through to its use and retirement.

## An example

A state skills department plans to use an AI tool to match job seekers with training courses. Before launch, the team checks whether the tool gives women and rural applicants poorer matches than others, explains its suggestions in plain language, stores only the data it needs, and names an officer who handles complaints. They also arrange for a person to review any case where an applicant disputes a recommendation.

## Why it matters

AI systems can make mistakes at large scale and affect people who never chose to use them. For businesses, responsible practice reduces legal, financial and reputational risk. For government bodies, it protects public trust and citizens' rights. For students and professionals, these skills are increasingly expected in AI and Data Science roles, because building a model is only part of the job.

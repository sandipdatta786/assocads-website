## In plain English

A neural network is a type of model loosely inspired by how brain cells connect, though it works very differently from a real brain. It is made of small units, often called neurons, arranged in layers. Each unit takes in numbers, combines them using weights, and passes a result on to the next layer.

During training, the network is shown many examples with known answers. Each time it gets one wrong, its weights are adjusted slightly. After many rounds, the network becomes good at turning inputs, such as the pixels of an image, into outputs, such as a label.

Networks with many layers are called deep neural networks, which is where the term deep learning comes from.

## An example

A postal sorting centre wants to read handwritten PIN codes on envelopes automatically. Engineers train a neural network on many scanned images of handwritten digits, each labelled with the correct number. Over time the network learns to recognise the shapes of each digit in different handwriting styles. Envelopes it cannot read with confidence are sent to a staff member to check.

## Why it matters

Neural networks are the building blocks of most modern AI, including image recognition, speech tools and large language models. Students in AI and Data Science need a working understanding of them. For professionals and organisations, knowing that these models learn from examples, rather than following written rules, helps explain both their power and their weaknesses, such as poor performance on unfamiliar data and decisions that are hard to explain.

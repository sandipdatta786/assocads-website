## In plain English

Many modern AI models, especially large neural networks, work in ways that are hard for people to follow. Explainable AI is the effort to make their behaviour understandable, both in general and for individual decisions.

NIST, the US standards body, sets out four principles. A system should offer an explanation for its outputs. The explanation should be meaningful to the person receiving it. It should accurately reflect how the output was actually produced. And the system should recognise the limits of what it was designed to do. NIST's risk management framework also distinguishes explainability, which is about how a system works, from interpretability, which is about what an output means in context.

## An example

A hospital in Kolkata trials a model that flags chest X-rays for urgent review. A radiologist is shown not only the flag but also a highlighted area of the image that most influenced the model, and a note when the image is of a type the model was not trained on. This helps the doctor decide whether to trust the flag, rather than accepting or ignoring it blindly.

## Why it matters

People are more likely to use AI sensibly when they can see why it reached a result. Explanations help developers find errors, help auditors and regulators check systems, and help the people affected question decisions that seem wrong.

The right kind of explanation depends on the audience. A data scientist, a doctor and a loan applicant need different levels of detail, so explainability is as much about clear communication as about technique.

## In plain English

A large language model, or LLM, is the technology behind most AI chat assistants. It has been trained on a very large body of text to do one basic thing: guess what comes next. It breaks text into small pieces called tokens, which can be words or parts of words, and predicts the most likely next token again and again.

Done at large scale, this simple task produces a system that can write paragraphs, answer questions, summarise documents and translate between languages. "Large" refers to the amount of training text and the number of internal settings, or parameters, the model has.

An LLM does not look facts up unless it is connected to a source. It can state wrong information confidently, and it can repeat biases found in its training text.

## An example

A district hospital receives patient feedback forms in English, Hindi and Bengali. Staff use an LLM-based tool to translate the comments into English and group them by theme, such as waiting times or cleanliness. A staff member reviews a sample of the translations against the originals to make sure the meaning has been kept before the summary goes to the management committee.

## Why it matters

LLMs are now part of search tools, office software and customer service systems, so most professionals will meet them. Students in AI and Data Science need to understand how they work to build on them or evaluate them. For organisations and government bodies, knowing their limits, including errors, bias and weaker performance in some Indian languages, is essential before using them in any service that affects people.

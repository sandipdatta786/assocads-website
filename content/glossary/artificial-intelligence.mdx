## In plain English

Artificial intelligence, or AI, is a broad name for computer systems that do tasks we usually link with human thinking. These tasks include recognising speech, reading images, translating text, spotting patterns and making suggestions.

Most AI today does not think like a person. It works out patterns from large amounts of data and uses them to produce an output for a new case. The OECD describes an AI system as a machine-based system that infers from its inputs how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions.

AI is an umbrella term. Machine learning, deep learning and generative AI are all parts of it.

## An example

A farmer in Bardhaman notices spots on the leaves of a paddy crop and takes a photograph with a phone. An AI system compares the photo with patterns it has learnt from many labelled images and suggests which pest or disease is most likely. An agricultural extension worker then checks the suggestion and advises on treatment. The AI did not decide anything on its own; it gave a trained person a fast first opinion.

## Why it matters

AI is now built into public services, banking, healthcare, farming and education in India, so most people are affected by it whether they notice or not. Students need to understand it to work in almost any technical field. Professionals and businesses need to know what it can and cannot do before they rely on it. Government bodies need a shared understanding of the term to write sensible rules and to buy AI systems wisely.

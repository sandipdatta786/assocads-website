## In plain English

AI governance is about who decides how AI may be used, under what rules, and how those rules are checked. It works at two levels. At the national level it covers laws, regulators, standards and public guidelines. Inside an organisation it covers policies, approval steps, risk reviews and named people who are responsible.

Good governance does not mean blocking AI. It means matching the level of control to the level of risk, so that a film recommendation tool is treated differently from a tool that helps decide who gets a loan or a medical referral.

In November 2025 the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology released the India AI Governance Guidelines, which set out seven guiding principles and recommendations for industry, developers and regulators.

## An example

A private hospital group in Kolkata wants to introduce an AI tool that reads chest X-rays. Its AI governance process requires a written risk assessment, testing on the hospital's own patient images, a check that patient data is handled lawfully, sign-off by a clinical committee, and a plan to monitor errors after launch. A radiologist remains responsible for every report.

## Why it matters

As AI spreads into decisions that affect people's health, money and rights, someone must be answerable for how it behaves. Government bodies need governance to protect citizens without stopping useful innovation. Businesses need it to manage legal and reputational risk and to win customers' trust. Professionals increasingly work under these rules, and students entering AI and Data Science will be expected to understand them.

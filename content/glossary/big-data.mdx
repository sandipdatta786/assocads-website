## In plain English

Big data is not simply "a lot of data". The term describes data that is too large, arrives too quickly or comes in too many forms for a single ordinary computer or database to handle well. Working with it usually means spreading the data and the processing across many machines.

NIST describes four characteristics, often called the four Vs. Volume is the size of the data. Velocity is how fast it flows in. Variety means it comes from many sources and in many formats. Variability means its speed or structure changes over time. Other "Vs" are sometimes added, but NIST treats these four as the ones that force a different kind of system design.

## An example

Think of a city transport authority that collects location pings from thousands of buses every few seconds, ticket transactions, traffic camera feeds and passenger complaints in several languages. The volume, speed and mix of formats are too much for one database. The data is stored and processed across a cluster of machines so that planners can see delays as they happen and study patterns over months.

## Why it matters

Large-scale data underpins much of modern AI, public digital services and business analytics. Understanding what makes data "big" helps students and professionals choose sensible tools rather than reaching for complex systems when a simple database would do.

For government and industry, big data also brings responsibilities. Large, linked datasets about people raise privacy, security and governance questions, and the cost of storing and processing them needs to be weighed against the value they actually provide.
